Reliability

A Silent Mount Is Not a Success

An evening log showed the program announcing “Slew completed” for a slew that had just been aborted. Reading the code for the cause found the same mistake in many places: a mount that does not answer was being read as a mount that has arrived.
By Bogdan • October 13, 2026 • DEV LOG • v1.4.10 PRO

Places That Ask the Mount

19
call sites that ask whether the mount is still slewing

Most of them treated an unanswered question the same as “no”. The ones that decide what happens next — plate solve, sync, flip, imaging — are fixed in 1.4.10.

The request was broad: audit the interface, the connections and the settings, and find the communication errors with the mount and the accessories. We started where the evidence was, in the log files of real evenings, and then read the code behind every message that looked wrong.

The Evening Log

One evening ended with an abort in the middle of a GOTO. The log, a moment later, said that the slew had completed. It had not: the mount had been told to stop. The program then went on as if it had arrived — it took a picture, solved it and corrected the mount’s idea of where it was pointing.

One Question, Three Answers

The wait loop asks the mount a yes-or-no question: are you still slewing? The answer comes back as yes, no or nothing, because the link can fail, and after two failed attempts the wrapper returns nothing. The loops were written as “keep waiting while the answer is yes”. Nothing ended the wait exactly like no.

while (slewing == true) wait();   // nothing  =>  stop waiting
announce("Slew completed");

The consequence is worst where it is least visible. A mount that has lost its link is, to the program, a mount that has arrived. A plate solve then compares the sky to a position the telescope never reached, and a sync writes the difference into the mount as if it were a pointing error.

A mount that does not answer has not arrived. It has not done anything we know of. On a question with three answers and a loop that heard two

The Repair

An abort is now remembered by the mount wrapper, and a wait loop that sees one stops with a warning and syncs nothing. A single unanswered question is tolerated, since drivers do glitch; three in a row mean the link is lost, and the operation stops with that message. The same rule now governs the scheduler’s slews, its meridian flip, the dither, the re-slew after a sync, the flip of the Imaging tab, Center-and-Rotate, three-point polar alignment and Just-Go.

The Window That Waited for the Mount

Two buttons — the sub-exposure calculator and the start of an imaging sequence — asked the mount for the focal length and then blocked the interface thread until it answered. The mount’s timeout is ten seconds. With a mount that is not answering, the window froze for ten seconds per question. They now wait without holding the window.

Accessories: Waiting for the Wrong Thing

The same family of defect turned up in the accessories, in three forms.

A wait that ends silently. The focuser waited sixty seconds for a move to finish and then reported Move complete, even when the motor was still turning. It now halts and reports a timeout.

An error read as “finished”. The rotator’s IsMoving returned false when the driver threw, which reads as a completed move. Three failed reads in a row are now an error and the rotator is halted. Its fallback to a second kind of move, meant for drivers without a mechanical move, also fired on any driver error and so could command a second movement; it now fires only when the driver lacks the method.

A wait that starts too early. The filter wheel returned as soon as it saw no motion, which can be before the motion has begun; it now waits for the wheel to report the slot that was asked for, and gives up quickly on a driver that stopped answering instead of waiting thirty seconds. The dome’s park and find-home, its shutter, and the flat panel’s cover had the same race and now confirm the state they were waiting for. A flat panel that never becomes ready is an error instead of a series of flats taken with the panel off.

The Derotator and Longitude Zero

The field derotator read the site’s latitude and longitude from the mount. When the longitude could not be read, it used zero — Greenwich — without a word, and rotated the camera accordingly. It now refuses to start and says why. Stopping the derotator also halts a rotator that was still turning, and the loop warns after five cycles without a position from the mount.

A Port That Answers Nobody

An LX200 mount’s Connect opened the serial port, sent a product query and returned success even if nothing replied. Any open COM port was a mount. It now fails when the query is not answered, and releases the port.

How We Know, and What We Do Not

For the focuser, the rotator and the filter wheel, a small stand-in for the driver was written, and the rotator and filter-wheel tests fail on the old code and pass on the new: a dead driver, a move that never ends, a wheel that reports the old slot. The GOTO loops, the scheduler and the flips are pinned by tests that read the source, because exercising them needs a window or a serial port. The whole suite is green.

What none of it proves is the behaviour with a real mount whose link is cut in the middle of a slew. The dome and the cover have no behavioural test. The polling timers of the focuser, rotator and filter wheel still read their driver on the interface thread — the drivers are created there, and moving them is a larger change. A driver that hangs can still freeze the window.

What Changed

Silence from the mount stops a GOTO, a flip, a dither and a polar alignment instead of ending them. Aborts are never synced. Moves of the focuser, rotator, wheel, dome and cover are confirmed or halted. A longitude that cannot be read stops the derotator. An LX200 that does not answer is not connected.

What Didn’t

Nothing was tried on a real mount with its link cut. The polling of three accessories still runs on the interface thread. Stacking, calibration and every image result are untouched.

The program can only act on what the hardware says. When the hardware says nothing, the honest report is that it said nothing, and that is what 1.4.10 now writes.
