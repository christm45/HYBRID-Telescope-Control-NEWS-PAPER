Safety

The Pole, the Sun and the Pier

The second half of the October audit: a safety monitor that parked the mount while the tracking loop kept pushing it, a polar alignment that aimed at the year 2000, and a request for the Sun that slewed to a galaxy.
By Bogdan • October 25, 2026 • DEV LOG • after v1.4.11 PRO

Polar Alignment

~9′
between the J2000 pole and today’s pole

The three-point alignment compared the mount’s axis with the pole of the year 2000. Its quality grades start at 30″. A perfectly aligned mount read “Poor”, and following the advice would have moved it nine arcminutes off.

The first half of this audit was about buttons that claimed success. This half is about the moments when the program has to protect the telescope, find the pole, or find an object by name — and about how a few of those were quietly wrong.

Parking While Something Else Is Steering

When an ASCOM safety monitor reports unsafe conditions, the program stops the scheduler and the imaging sequence and parks the mount. It did not stop the closed-loop tracking corrections, model-rate tracking, PHD2 guiding or dome slaving. On many drivers a rate correction or a guide pulse during the park slew aborts the park — and a roll-off roof will not close over a mount that is not parked. Dome slaving, meanwhile, kept turning the dome towards the mount while the park drove it home. All four are now stopped before the park. A rotating dome’s shutter still starts closing first, since stopping the loops can take a few seconds; the independent review of this fix caught that the first version had delayed it.

A weather station connected over ASCOM Alpaca was only being read for its sky-quality value. Its rain and wind readings now trigger the same stop-and-park after two bad readings in a row; humidity, cloud and dew limits only warn, because they are common at night and not dangerous.

A Failed Flip Is Not a Reason to Carry On

When a meridian flip failed — the pier side did not change, or the slew had not finished after five minutes — the flip manager switched tracking off and then resumed the imaging sequence anyway. A slew still running after five minutes was not even stopped. Now the slew is aborted, imaging stays paused, and the message says so. The “pause N minutes before the flip” setting, which nothing used to read, now holds the sequence between frames until the target has crossed the meridian. A manual flip used to wait two seconds for “the current exposure to finish”; it now waits for the exposure itself.

Polar Alignment at 45° North, in the Year 2000

The polar-alignment window looked for its latitude in an interface field that does not exist, so it always ran at 45° north, and it was never given a longitude — which the code’s own comment says gives the wrong knob directions about half the time. It compared the mount’s axis with the J2000 pole. And if the mount refused one of the three rotations, the next picture showed the same field, the three positions nearly coincided, and the result could read as a perfect 0″. It now takes the site from the mount or the Weather tab and refuses to run on a placeholder site, precesses each solved position to the equinox of date, and stops with a clear message when a rotation is refused or the three positions are too close together to define an axis.

“Go to the Sun”

Object names were searched catalogue by catalogue, Messier first, partial matches included. “Sun” is part of “Sunflower Galaxy”, so a remote request for the Sun returned M63 — and the solar-safety check, which refuses the Sun, never saw it. An exact name in any catalogue now wins over a partial one, and partial matches must be whole words; apostrophes and hyphens are ignored, so “cats eye” still finds the Cat’s Eye.

The Caldwell, Herschel 400 and Collinder catalogues had their constellation table shifted — Cepheus showed as Circinus, Cassiopeia as Cetus — and every galaxy was labelled “Unknown”. The table was rebuilt from the catalogue files themselves, whose comments name every index; it follows the byte order of the abbreviations, so CMa comes before Cae. The objects could not be found by voice either, because the lookup was case-sensitive.

Smaller, but Real

Periodic error. The PEC export wrote the measured error where the file promises the correction, so uploading it would have doubled the error; and the worm period could only come out as 512 s or 341 s for the 12-minute logs the program asks for. Both are fixed, and a 400, 480 or 600 s worm is now found within 3 %.

Horizon. The safety horizon used 0 as “no point found”, but 0° is north; every sector next to north fell back to the global 10° instead of the profile’s horizon.

Calibration. “Darks before, flats after” shot the whole calibration set twice, flats included at night. The calibration wizard took its dark-flats at a fixed 0.5 s whatever the flats used, and never sent the chosen binning or offset to the camera.

Mosaic. The end of each panel was treated as the end of imaging, which hid the Stop button for the remaining panels, and a Stop pressed during a slew was ignored for the whole next panel.

The rest. Crash recovery was never offered after a real crash. The web password could not be changed, although every message said where to do it. An overnight batch queue stopped after its first job, waiting for a click on a “job completed” box.

How We Know, and What We Do Not

The pier-side convention was measured on the ASCOM simulator rather than assumed. The catalogue, PEC, horizon, calibration-timing and mosaic fixes have tests that fail on the old code and pass on the new. The safety, flip and dialog fixes are pinned by tests that read the source, because exercising them needs a mount and a window. A second independent review of this batch found several more problems in the fixes themselves — including the delayed dome shutter and a polar alignment that would have accepted the placeholder site — and they were corrected before the commit.

What Changed

Unsafe conditions stop every axis driver before the park. A failed flip stops instead of resuming. Polar alignment uses the real site and today’s pole. “Sun” is the Sun. The SmartHand catalogues are labelled correctly. PEC, horizon, calibration and mosaic do what their settings say.

What Didn’t

None of it has been tried under a real sky or on a real dome. The licence check remains symmetric and is waiting for a decision, since changing it would invalidate existing keys. These fixes are in the source and will ship in the next installer, not in v1.4.11.

Each of these was a small, plausible-looking line. Together they are the difference between a program that parks the telescope and one that only says it did.
