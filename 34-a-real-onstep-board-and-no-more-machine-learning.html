Tracking

On a Real OnStep Board: Session Learning Replaces the Machine-Learning Layer

The closed tracking loop was audited, tested end to end, then run against a real OnStep controller board driving an alt-az mount. The machine-learning layer, measured to change tracking by 0 %, was removed; what replaces it learns physical properties of the mount during each session.
By Bogdan • November 9, 2026 • DEV LOG • v1.4.15 PRO

Machine-Learning Layer

0 %
measured effect on tracking

Measured on the closed loop: between −1 % and +3 %, within the noise. It mainly scaled the filter’s correction down, most of all in wind. Removed in v1.4.15.

Dispatches 03, 11 and 15 described a machine-learning layer, an ML.NET (SDCA) regression blended with the Kalman filter. An audit of the whole tracking chain measured what it did: an effect between −1 % and +3 %, a coefficient of determination of 0.26 at best, a single model shared by every mount, and an uncertainty input in the wrong units. The layer was switched off and its code and libraries removed. A change made after v1.4.17, for the next installer, also deletes the ML.NET libraries that an upgrade left behind.

What Is Learned Instead

During each session, from the plate solves themselves, the loop now learns: the mount’s real response on each axis, that is how far the axis moves per unit of correction (bounded between 0.33 and 3); the noise of the plate solves; an axis that moves in reverse, for example after a driver setting or a meridian flip, which is detected by a quick check, corrected, then locked; and whether a periodic-error model on the worm period actually fits, decided by comparison with a model without it. What has been learned is shown in a new Learning tab and on the web remote.

The machine-learning layer did not add a prediction; it turned the filter’s correction down, most of all in wind. On what the measurement showed

Tested End to End Before the Board

A new end-to-end suite runs the real tracking loop against a simulated mount that physically executes rate commands, MoveAxis and guide pulses. Eleven scenarios pass: three correction channels, a mount that moves 2.5 times more than commanded, a reversed declination axis, clouds and false plate solves, refused writes, a runaway, interleaved imaging with dither, and an emergency stop. A Monte Carlo run over 1,200 configurations counted 331 divergences with the old law and 3 with the new one, all three of which also diverge with the old law; the median error fell by 33 to 37 %.

On the Board

The loop was then run against a real OnStep 4.25_B controller on an ESP32 board driving an alt-az mount, through its ASCOM driver over USB and Wi-Fi. The plate solves were simulated; the mount was real. The board has no RA/Dec rate properties, so HYBRID corrects with guide pulses. The alt-az filter was found to copy its measurements (a normalised innovation of 0.02 instead of about 2); with the commanded motion as an input and the learned plate-solve noise, it now behaves as a filter.

On this board, a pulse that slowed an axis added a fixed jump of about 4.4″, whatever its length. The cause was in the board’s firmware, whose motor timer kept a long period armed until it expired; it was corrected in the firmware. On HYBRID’s side, mounts corrected by guide pulses now guide at a lower rate during the loop — 0.25× by default, under Configuration > Closed-Loop Guiding. On the board the star moved 0.58–0.60″ at 0.25× against 0.76–0.80″ at 1×. The mount’s own guide rate is saved first and always restored, even after a crash. The closed loop on the board finished at 0.9–1.1″ RMS.

A Monitor for Guide Pulses, and Other Fixes

A guide-pulse monitor names a pulse direction that jumps, does less than asked, or moves the other axis. It detected a simulated 2″ jump; the absence of false alarms on the real board has not yet been verified. Other fixes: a runaway loop stops automatically after 15 minutes without a usable plate solve; tracking captures are deleted (they could reach 135 GB in a night); an emergency stop now stops every loop; every GOTO, park or home stops the loop except an announced dither; and the export of the periodic error to the mount’s firmware was removed because the phase of the worm was not known.

What Changed

Machine-learning layer removed; per-session learning of axis response, plate-solve noise, axis direction and periodic error; Learning tab; reduced guide rate during the loop, always restored; guide-pulse monitor; end-to-end and Monte Carlo tests.

What Didn’t

The plate solves on the board were simulated. Nothing in this release has run under a real sky. The board bench is one alt-az mount and one firmware.
